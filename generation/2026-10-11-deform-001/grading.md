### Prior art, searched after computing

- **Chryssomalakos and Okon (2004)**, "Generalized quantum relativistic kinematics: a stability point of view", arXiv:hep-th/0410212, Int. J. Mod. Phys. D 13: the same three-dimensional deformation space of the Poincare-Heisenberg algebra, an "instability double cone through its origin" given by alpha_3^2 = alpha_1 alpha_2 (eq. 82), six isomorphism classes (two open interiors, the exterior, the two nappes and the apex), and off the cone so(m, 6 - m) by region: so(1,5), so(2,4), so(3,3) (eq. 90). Their cone is this round's D = 0 up to the normalization of the cocycles (their sign conventions put so(2,4) where alpha_1 alpha_2 < 0; this round's put so(4,2) where ab > 0 at c = 0: **inferred**, from comparing the regions, not from mapping the bases). They also wrote "some modest computer code" for the search. **read**, `excerpts/arxiv-hep-th-0410212.txt`.
- **Vilela Mendes (1994)**, J. Phys. A 27, 8091: the relativistic quantum algebra is unstable and admits a two-parameter stabilizing deformation (the abstract; **inspected-abstract**, scanned PDF). Chryssomalakos and Okon credit it as their motivation and say the double cone is not mentioned there (**read**).
- Khruschev and Leznov (2002, arXiv:hep-th/0207082) are cited by Chryssomalakos and Okon for the so(m, 6 - m) isomorphism and for treating the third parameter on an equal footing (**secondary**; queued).

**Verdict on novelty: dead.** The seed's result is a rediscovery of Chryssomalakos and Okon (2004), itself following Vilela Mendes (1994). What survives is the calibration value: run blind from structure constants, the generator reproduced a published classification, including the so(5,1) region the leads had missed, and the leads' error was caught by the computation, not by the literature.

### The so(4,2) isomorphisms

so(4,2) is the conformal algebra of Minkowski space and the isometry algebra of AdS5; so(5,1) is the conformal algebra of Euclidean four-space (all **recalled**). These are isomorphisms of abstract Lie algebras. That direction (c) turns I into a generator acting as a dilatation is algebraic; that the deformed algebra is "the conformal group" or "AdS5" of anything physical is **expression only**, a reading this round does not earn. No physical claim rests on it.

## 5. Scope

- The seed and every template were chosen by hand. The generator decides what a template can do, not which templates to try; the invariant H^2 computation is what shows the hand-chosen directions span everything (rank 4 of 4, rank 3 of 3).
- Only deformations invariant under the rotations (calibration) or the Lorentz algebra (seed) were explored as directions. Here that loses nothing, since the full H^2 equals the invariant one in both rounds, but a seed whose symmetry subalgebra is not semisimple would break that equality.
- Integration searched one ray at a time, and the family's exactness off the grid rests on the polynomial argument above. The sign grid is finite: the three-region reading is checked at 52 points and confirmed by prior art, not proved here.
- The method is finite-dimensional. It cannot touch infinite-dimensional symmetry: diffeomorphisms, the Virasoro or BMS algebras, the Moyal deformation of the Poisson algebra (classical to quantum), or any field theory's gauge algebra. The calibration's history is the part of physics that lives in finite dimensions.
- The reading of theory change as deformation is expression. The calibration shows the reading generates correct algebra; whether superseded theories are in general deformations of their successors' algebras is not tested by two rounds.
