## D3-001 Cut-elimination (Gentzen's Hauptsatz)

*Transformer; surgery, computation-bridge; origin: map; tier: landed*

Algorithmically removes all uses of the cut rule from a sequent calculus proof. Reduces cuts on complex formulas to cuts on subformulas until eliminated. Result is analytic: every formula in proof is subformula of conclusion (subformula property). Foundational for consistency proofs.

- input: Sequent calculus proof with cut rules
- output: Cut-free proof of same sequent
- preserved: Provability, subformula property achieved
- broken: Cut rule applications, proof length (may increase super-exponentially)
- complexity: Upper bound: tower of exponentials in proof length; hyper-exponential in general

## D3-002 Proof normalization

*Transformer; surgery; origin: map; tier: audited (imprecise)*

Eliminates deductive detours in natural deduction: consecutive intro-elim pairs for same connective. Yields canonical form. Guarantees logical harmony.

- input: Natural deduction proof with intro-elim detours
- output: Normal-form proof
- preserved: Provability, logical harmony
- broken: Detour structure, proof length (may increase)
- complexity: Depends on logic; strongly normalizing for typed lambda calculi

## D3-003 β-reduction

*Transformer; surgery, local; origin: map; tier: landed*

Function application in lambda calculus: (λx.M)N -> M[N/x]. Under Curry-Howard: normalization of proof = execution of program.

- input: Lambda term (λx.M)N (redex)
- output: Term M[N/x] (contractum)
- preserved: Typing (subject reduction), observational equivalence
- broken: Redex structure, term size (may increase temporarily)
- complexity: O(1) per step; total steps may be non-elementary

## D3-004 η-reduction

- input: Lambda term λx.Mx (eta-redex)
- output: Term M
- preserved: Typing, extensional equivalence
- broken: Eta-redex wrapper, intensional distinction
- complexity: O(1) per step

## D3-005 Strong normalization

*Transformer; closure; origin: map; tier: landed*

## D3-012 Negative translation (general)

*Transformer; representation-change; origin: map; tier: landed*

Embeds classical logic into intuitionistic by inserting double negations. Proves relative consistency: if intuitionistic logic is consistent, so is classical.

- input: Classical formula/proof
- output: Intuitionistic formula/proof (double-negation translated)
- preserved: Validity (classical valid iff translated intuitionistically valid), relative consistency
- broken: Constructive content (hidden behind double negations)
- complexity: O(|φ|) where |φ| = formula size
