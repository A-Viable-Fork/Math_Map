factors a [[matrix (mathematics)|matrix]] as the product of a lower [[triangular matrix]] and an upper triangular matrix

LU decomposition can be viewed as the matrix form of [[Gaussian elimination]].

Let {{mvar|A}} be a square matrix. An '''LU factorization''' refers to expression of {{mvar|A}} into product of two factors – a lower triangular matrix {{mvar|L}} and an upper triangular matrix {{mvar|U}} such that {{math|1=''A'' = ''LU''}}. Sometimes factorization is impossible without prior reordering of {{mvar|A}} to prevent [[division by zero]] or uncontrolled growth of rounding errors.

It turns out that a proper permutation of rows (or columns) to select column (or row) absolute maximal pivot {{math|''a''{{sub|11}}}} is sufficient for numerically stable LU factorization, except for known pathological cases.

<math display=block> PA = LU, \quad (AQ=LU),</math>

which, when correspondingly left- and right-multiplied to {{mvar|A}}, reorder the rows and columns of {{mvar|A}}. It turns out that all square matrices can be factorized in this form,

and the factorization is numerically stable in practice.

where {{mvar|D}} is a [[diagonal matrix]], and {{mvar|L}} and {{mvar|U}} are [[Triangular matrix#Unitriangular matrix|unitriangular matrices]], meaning that all the entries on the diagonals of {{mvar|L}} and {{mvar|U}} are one.

<math display=block> A = LDU, </math>

If {{mvar|A}} is [[invertible matrix|invertible]], then it admits an LU (or LDU) factorization [[if and only if]] all its leading principal [[minor (linear algebra)|minors]] are nonzero
