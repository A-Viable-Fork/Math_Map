an '''exact sequence''' is a sequence of [[morphisms]] between objects (for example, [[Group (mathematics)|groups]], [[Module (mathematics)|modules]], and, more generally, objects of an [[abelian category]]) such that the [[Image (category theory)|image]] of one morphism equals the [[kernel (category theory)|kernel]] of the next.

of groups and [[group homomorphism]]s is said to be '''exact''' '''at''' <math>G_i</math> if <math>\operatorname{im}(f_i)=\ker(f_{i+1})</math>.

The [[snake lemma]] shows how a [[commutative diagram]] with two exact rows gives rise to a longer exact sequence.
