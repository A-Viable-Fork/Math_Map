'''Kähler differentials''' provide an adaptation of [[differential form]]s to arbitrary [[commutative ring]]s or [[scheme (mathematics)|scheme]]s.

Let {{math|''R''}} and {{math|''S''}} be commutative rings and {{math|''φ'' : ''R'' → ''S''}} be a [[ring homomorphism]].

An important example is for {{math|''R''}} a [[Field (mathematics)|field]] and {{math|''S''}} a unital [[associative algebra|algebra]] over {{math|''R''}}

The [[module (mathematics)|module]] of Kähler differentials is defined as the {{math|''S''}}-module <math>\Omega_{S/R}</math> for which there is a universal derivation <math>d : S \to \Omega_{S/R}</math>.

:<math>\operatorname{Hom}_S(\Omega_{S/R},M) \xrightarrow{\cong} \operatorname{Der}_R(S,M).</math>
