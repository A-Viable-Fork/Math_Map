Bousfield localization is named after [[Aldridge Bousfield]], who first introduced this technique in the context of localization of [[topological space|topological spaces]] and spectra.

replaces the model structure with another model structure with the same cofibrations but with more weak equivalences.

Given a [[class (mathematics)|class]] ''C'' of morphisms in a [[model category]] ''M''

the left Bousfield localization is a new model structure on the same category as before.

* the original cofibrations of ''M''

is required to be a weak equivalence (of [[simplicial set]]s) for any ''C''-local object ''W''.
