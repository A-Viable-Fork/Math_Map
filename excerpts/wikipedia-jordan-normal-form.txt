The diagonal form for [[diagonalizable]] matrices, for instance [[normal matrix|normal matrices]], is a special case of the Jordan normal form.

So there exists an invertible matrix ''P'' such that ''P''<sup>−1</sup>''AP'' = ''J'' is such that the only non-zero entries of ''J'' are on the diagonal and the superdiagonal. ''J'' is called the '''Jordan normal form''' of ''A''.

* Counting multiplicities, the eigenvalues of ''J'', and therefore of ''A'', are the diagonal entries.

it is the number of Jordan blocks corresponding to ''λ''<sub>''i''</sub>.

* The sum of the sizes of all Jordan blocks corresponding to an eigenvalue ''λ''<sub>''i''</sub> is its [[algebraic multiplicity]].

* ''A'' is diagonalizable if and only if, for every eigenvalue ''λ'' of ''A'', its geometric and algebraic multiplicities coincide. In particular, the Jordan blocks in this case are {{val|1|×|1}} matrices; that is, scalars.

The Jordan form of a ''n'' × ''n'' matrix ''A'' is block diagonal, and therefore gives a decomposition of the ''n'' dimensional Euclidean space into invariant subspaces of ''A''.
