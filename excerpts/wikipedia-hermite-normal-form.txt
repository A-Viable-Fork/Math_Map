the '''Hermite normal form''' is an analogue of [[reduced echelon form]] for [[matrix (mathematics)|matrices]] over the [[integer]]s

A matrix <math>A \in \mathbb{Z}^{m \times n}</math> has a (row) Hermite normal form <math>H</math> if there is a square [[unimodular matrix]] <math>U</math> such that <math>H=UA</math>

# <math>H</math> is upper triangular (that is, <math>h_{ij}=0</math> for <math>i>j</math>), and any rows of zeros are located below any other row.

# The elements below pivots are zero and elements above pivots are nonnegative and strictly smaller than the pivot.

A unimodular matrix is a square [[integer matrix]] whose [[determinant]] is 1 or −1 (and hence [[Invertible matrix|invertible]]). In fact, a unimodular matrix is invertible over the integers

Every full row rank ''m''-by-''n'' matrix '''A''' with integer entries has a unique ''m''-by-''n'' matrix '''H''' in Hermite normal form, such that '''H'''='''UA''' for some square unimodular matrix '''U'''.

Because the Hermite normal form is unique, it can be used to answer many questions about two lattice descriptions.
