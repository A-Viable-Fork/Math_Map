a '''Tschirnhaus transformation''', also known as '''Tschirnhausen transformation''', is a type of mapping on [[polynomial]]s

it is a method for transforming a polynomial equation of degree <math>n\ge2</math> with some nonzero intermediate coefficients, <math>a_1, ..., a_{n-1}</math>, such that some or all of the transformed intermediate coefficients, <math>a'_1, ..., a'_{n-1}</math>, are exactly zero.

For example, finding a substitution<math display="block">y(x)=k_1x^2 + k_2x+k_3</math>

as the transformation on [[Minimal polynomial (field theory)|minimal polynomials]] implied by a different choice of [[primitive element (field theory)|primitive element]].

The roots of the original polynomial <math>f</math> may be obtained from the roots of this transformed polynomial <math>f'</math> by the same transformation.

In 1786, this work was expanded by [[Erland Samuel Bring]] who showed that any generic quintic polynomial could be similarly reduced.

This concept is used in reducing quintics to [[Bring radical#Bring–Jerrard normal form|Bring&ndash;Jerrard form]]
