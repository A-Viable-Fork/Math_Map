an '''adjunction space''' (or '''attaching space''') is a common construction in [[topology]] where one [[topological space]] is attached or "glued" onto another.

Specifically, let <math>X</math> and <math>Y</math> be topological spaces, and let <math>A</math> be a [[subspace (topology)|subspace]] of <math>Y</math>. Let <math>f : A \rightarrow X</math> be a [[continuous (topology)|continuous map]] (called the '''attaching map''').

<math display="block">X\cup_f Y = (X\sqcup Y) / \sim</math>

where the [[equivalence relation]] <math> \sim</math> is generated by <math> a\sim f(a)</math> for all <math>a</math> in <math>A</math>

Inductively attaching cells along their spherical boundaries to this space results in an example of a [[CW complex]].
