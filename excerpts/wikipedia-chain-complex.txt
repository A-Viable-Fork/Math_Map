A '''chain complex''' <math>(A_\bullet, d_\bullet)</math> is a sequence of abelian groups or modules

Explicitly, the differentials satisfy <math>d_n\circ d_{n+1}=0</math> for all <math>n</math>, or, concisely, <math>d^2=0</math>.

connected by homomorphisms (called '''boundary operators''' or '''differentials''')

Associated to a chain complex is its [[Homology (mathematics)|homology]]

connected by homomorphisms ('''coboundary operators''') <math>d^n:A^n\to A^{n+1}</math>

connected by homomorphisms ('''coboundary operators''') <math>d^n:A^n\to A^{n+1}</math> satisfying <math>d^{n+1}\circ d^{n}=0</mat

It consists of a sequence of abelian groups or modules <math>\cdots, A^0, A^1, A^2,\dots</math>

The homology of a cochain complex is called its [[cohomology]].

The elements in the kernel of <math>d</math> are called '''(co)cycles''' (or '''closed''' elements), and the elements in the image of ''d'' are called '''(co)boundaries''' (or '''exact''' elements).

satisfying <math>d^{n+1}\circ d^{n}=0</mat

Associated to a chain complex is its [[Homology (mathematics)|homology]], which is (loosely speaking) a measure of the failure of a chain complex to be [[Exact sequence|exact]].

and is a commonly used [[topological invariant|invariant]] of a topological space.
