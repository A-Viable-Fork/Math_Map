The '''modified discrete cosine transform''' ('''MDCT''') is a transform based on the type-IV [[discrete cosine transform]] (DCT-IV), with the additional property of being [[lapped transform|lapped]]

it is designed to be performed on consecutive blocks of a larger [[dataset]], where subsequent blocks are overlapped

<math>F\colon \mathbf{R}^{2N} \to \mathbf{R}^N</math>

it is designed to be performed on consecutive blocks of a larger [[dataset]]

it has half as many outputs as inputs (instead of the same number).

However, perfect invertibility is achieved by ''adding'' the overlapped IMDCTs of subsequent overlapping blocks

at first glance it might seem that the MDCT should not be invertible.

The MDCT was later proposed by John P. Princen, A.W. Johnson and Alan B. Bradley at the [[University of Surrey]] in 1987

following earlier work by Princen and Bradley (1986)
