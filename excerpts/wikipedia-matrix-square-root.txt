A matrix {{mvar|B}} is said to be a square root of {{mvar|A}} if the [[matrix product]] {{math|''BB''}} is equal to {{mvar|A}}.

has infinitely many square roots

But some matrices have no square root.

This unique matrix is called the '''principal''', '''non-negative''', or '''positive square root''' (the latter in the case of a [[positive definite matrix]]).

Another way to find the square root of an {{tmath|n \times n}} matrix {{tmath|A}} is the Denman–Beavers square root iteration.
