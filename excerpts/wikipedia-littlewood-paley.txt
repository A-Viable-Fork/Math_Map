Littlewood–Paley theory uses a decomposition of a function ''f'' into a sum of functions ''f''<sub>''&rho;''</sub> with localized frequencies.

this gives a so-called "dyadic decomposition" of ''f''

It is typically used as a substitute for orthogonality arguments which only apply to ''L''<sup>''p''</sup> functions when ''p''&nbsp;=&nbsp;2.
