Any rotation is a [[motion (geometry)|motion]] of a certain [[space (mathematics)|space]] that preserves at least one [[point (geometry)|point]].

This (common) fixed point or [[center (geometry)|center]] is called the ''[[center of rotation]]''

* The ''[[axis of rotation]]'' is a [[line (geometry)|line]] of its fixed points.

A motion of a [[Euclidean space]] is the same as its [[isometry]]: it leaves [[Euclidean distance|the distance]] between any two points unchanged after the transformation. But a (proper) rotation also has to preserve the [[orientation (vector space)|orientation structure]].

In [[Plane (mathematics)|two dimensions]], only a single [[angle]] is needed to specify a rotation about the [[origin (mathematics)|origin]]
