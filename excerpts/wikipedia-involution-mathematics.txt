{{Short description|Function that is its own inverse}}

An involution is a function {{math|''f'' : ''X'' → ''X''}} that, when applied twice, brings one back to the starting point.

Given a module {{math|''M''}} over a [[ring (mathematics)|ring]] {{math|''R''}}, an {{math|''R''}} [[endomorphism]] {{math|''f''}} of {{math|''M''}} is called an involution if {{math|{{itco|''f''}}<sup>2</sup>}} is the identity homomorphism on {{math|''M''}}.

In [[ring theory]], the word ''involution'' is customarily taken to mean an [[antihomomorphism]] that is its own inverse function.
